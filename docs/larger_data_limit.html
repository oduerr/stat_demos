Bernstein–von Mises — watching skew dissolve into a Gaussian

The finite-n posterior is genuinely non-Gaussian: a skewed, positively-bounded Gamma on the rate θ. The gold BvM Gaussian is wrong at small n (it leaks past the boundary at 0) and becomes right as n→∞. Slide n up to 100 000 to watch both concentrate on the true θ₀ (consistency). yᵢ ~ Gamma(shape k=2, rate θ),  prior θ ~ Gamma(α=2, β=1)  ⟹  posterior θ | y ~ Gamma(kn+α, Σyᵢ+β),  I(θ₀)=k/θ₀²

At n = 0 the posterior is just the prior Gamma(2, 1). The exact posterior (blue) is right-skewed and lives only on θ>0. The BvM Gaussian (gold) is symmetric and spills below zero — visibly wrong at small n (red region = mass placed below 0, where the rate can't go). As n grows the Gamma's skewness 2/√(kn+α) → 0; blue and gold merge, the mass below 0 vanishes, and both collapse onto θ₀. That convergence is BvM.
